Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 13, 2026

Aurora is a private streaming service. It is made of the Aurora app (Android, Android TV and iPhone), this website, and a small server program called Aurora Guard that belongs to the person who runs your service. This page says what information those parts handle and what we do with it. In short: we use it to run your account and nothing else. There are no ads, no tracking tools and we never sell your information.

Who is responsible

Your Aurora service is run by an individual operator (the person or business that gave you access). They are responsible for the information described here. Ask the person who gave you access to Aurora if you have a question.

What we collect, and why

  • Your sign-in. The username and password you enter go to your media server to sign you in. The iPhone app does not store your password. It keeps a sign-in token on your phone so you don't have to sign in every time, and you remove it by signing out.
  • Your device. The type of device (for example "iPhone") and an ID the app makes up for itself are sent to the server, so your plan's limit on how many devices can use an account can be applied.
  • What you watch. Your server records what you play and where you stopped, so "Continue Watching" works. Aurora Guard keeps simple totals (such as minutes watched and which titles) for the operator, and the channels you report as not working.
  • Website sign-up. If you start a free trial on the website: a username, an email address, the discount or referral code you entered, and your internet address (IP address), which is used only to stop one person making many accounts.
  • Messages you send. If you ask for help or for more time on your plan, we keep what you write so the operator can answer.
  • Problem reports. If a screen goes wrong, a short log can be sent to the operator to help fix it. It can include your username and device type. It does not include your password.

What we do not do

  • We do not show ads and we do not use advertising or analytics tools in the app.
  • We do not sell or rent your information, and we do not share it for marketing.
  • We do not track you across other apps or websites.

Other services the app talks to

  • Your TV provider. If Live TV or the provider's movies and shows are set up, your device asks the provider directly for the video. The provider can see your internet address and what you request, under its own policy.
  • GitHub. The app and server look up their release notes and settings from GitHub. GitHub sees your internet address.
  • YouTube. If you tap a trailer, it plays from YouTube, under YouTube's policy.
  • Poster and picture sources. Posters and artwork are loaded from your server or from the picture sources it uses.

How long we keep it

We keep your account information while your account exists. Viewing totals and logs are kept only as long as they are needed to run the service. When an account is deleted, the account and its sign-in, viewing history and messages are removed from the server and from Aurora Guard.

Your choices

  • Look at, correct or delete your information. Ask the operator (see the contact above). We will remove your account and its information when you ask.
  • Sign out. Signing out of the app removes the sign-in token from your phone.
  • Turn things off. In Settings you can switch off optional features, such as sounds and the live picture in the Android guide.

Security

Connections to your server and Aurora Guard use HTTPS. Administrators of the service can see account details and viewing totals so they can run the service. We take reasonable care, but no system is perfectly secure.

Children

Aurora is not made for children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their information. Parents can use the Kids setting on an account to limit what is shown.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. Big changes are also announced in the app.

This page is a plain-language description of how Aurora works. It is not legal advice.
